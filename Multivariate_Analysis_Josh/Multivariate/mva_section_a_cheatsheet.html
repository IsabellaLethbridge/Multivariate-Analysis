Three decompositions — conceptual knowledge
▼
Eigendecomposition (symmetric PD) \[\mathbf{A}=\mathbf{U}\mathbf{D}\mathbf{U}'\quad(\mathbf{U}\text{ orthogonal})\]
Spectral decomposition \[\mathbf{A}=\sum_{i=1}^k\lambda_i\mathbf{e}_i\mathbf{e}_i'\qquad\mathbf{A}^{-1}=\sum\tfrac{1}{\lambda_i}\mathbf{e}_i\mathbf{e}_i'\qquad\mathbf{A}^{1/2}=\sum\sqrt{\lambda_i}\mathbf{e}_i\mathbf{e}_i'\]
SVD \[\mathbf{A}_{m\times k}=\mathbf{U}\boldsymbol{\Lambda}\mathbf{V}'=\sum_{i=1}^r\sqrt{\lambda_i}\,\mathbf{u}_i\mathbf{v}_i'\] \(\mathbf{U}\): eigenvectors of \(\mathbf{A}\mathbf{A}'\); \(\mathbf{V}\): eigenvectors of \(\mathbf{A}'\mathbf{A}\); non-zero eigenvalues equal.

§2.3 — Rank-\(s\) SVD is the best low-rank approximation
proof — must know
▼
Claim \(\mathbf{B}=\sum_{i=1}^s\sqrt{\lambda_i}\mathbf{u}_i\mathbf{v}_i'\) minimises \(\text{tr}[(\mathbf{A}-\mathbf{B})(\mathbf{A}-\mathbf{B})']\) over all rank-\(\leq s\) matrices.
Proof
  1. Insert \(\mathbf{U}\mathbf{U}'=\mathbf{I}_m\) and \(\mathbf{V}\mathbf{V}'=\mathbf{I}_k\): \[\text{tr}[(\mathbf{A}-\mathbf{B})(\mathbf{A}-\mathbf{B})'] = \text{tr}[(\boldsymbol{\Lambda}-\mathbf{C})(\boldsymbol{\Lambda}-\mathbf{C})']\] where \(\mathbf{C}=\mathbf{U}'\mathbf{B}\mathbf{V}\).
  2. Expand: \(\displaystyle\sum_{i,j}(\sqrt{\lambda_{ij}}-c_{ij})^2 = \underbrace{\sum_i(\sqrt{\lambda_i}-c_{ii})^2}_{\text{diagonal}} + \underbrace{\sum_{i\neq j}c_{ij}^2}_{\geq 0}\)
  3. Minimised when: \(c_{ij}=0\;\forall i\neq j\) and \(c_{ii}=\sqrt{\lambda_i}\) for \(s\) largest, \(0\) for rest.
  4. \(\therefore\;\mathbf{U}'\mathbf{B}\mathbf{V}=\boldsymbol{\Lambda}_s\;\Rightarrow\;\mathbf{B}=\sum_{i=1}^s\sqrt{\lambda_i}\mathbf{u}_i\mathbf{v}_i'\). \(\square\)

§3.3 — Generalised distance is \(\chi^2_p\) (exception from CA)
proof
NB
▼
\[(\mathbf{X}-\boldsymbol{\mu})'\boldsymbol{\Sigma}^{-1}(\mathbf{X}-\boldsymbol{\mu})\sim\chi^2_p\] \[\Pr\!\left[(\mathbf{x}-\boldsymbol{\mu})'\boldsymbol{\Sigma}^{-1}(\mathbf{x}-\boldsymbol{\mu})\leq\chi^2_p(\alpha)\right]=1-\alpha\]
Proof — spectral decomposition (Stefan: he may ask for a specific method)
  1. Write \(\boldsymbol{\Sigma}=\mathbf{P}\boldsymbol{\Lambda}\mathbf{P}'\) (spectral decomp, \(\mathbf{P}\) orthogonal).
  2. Let \(\mathbf{Y}=\boldsymbol{\Lambda}^{-1/2}\mathbf{P}'(\mathbf{X}-\boldsymbol{\mu})\). Then \(E(\mathbf{Y})=\mathbf{0}\) and \[\text{Cov}(\mathbf{Y})=\boldsymbol{\Lambda}^{-1/2}\mathbf{P}'\,\boldsymbol{\Sigma}\,\mathbf{P}\boldsymbol{\Lambda}^{-1/2}=\boldsymbol{\Lambda}^{-1/2}\boldsymbol{\Lambda}\boldsymbol{\Lambda}^{-1/2}=\mathbf{I}_p\] so \(\mathbf{Y}\sim N_p(\mathbf{0},\mathbf{I})\) — components i.i.d. \(N(0,1)\).
  3. \(\mathbf{Y}'\mathbf{Y}=\sum_{i=1}^p Y_i^2\sim\chi^2_p\).
  4. But \(\mathbf{Y}'\mathbf{Y}=(\mathbf{X}-\boldsymbol{\mu})'\mathbf{P}\boldsymbol{\Lambda}^{-1}\mathbf{P}'(\mathbf{X}-\boldsymbol{\mu})=(\mathbf{X}-\boldsymbol{\mu})'\boldsymbol{\Sigma}^{-1}(\mathbf{X}-\boldsymbol{\mu})\). \(\square\)
Properties §3.4.1–3.4.3 — used throughout
▼
Linear combinations are normal \(\mathbf{a}'\mathbf{X}\sim N(\mathbf{a}'\boldsymbol{\mu},\,\mathbf{a}'\boldsymbol{\Sigma}\mathbf{a})\) for any \(\mathbf{a}\). And \(\mathbf{A}\mathbf{X}\sim N_q(\mathbf{A}\boldsymbol{\mu},\,\mathbf{A}\boldsymbol{\Sigma}\mathbf{A}')\).
Subsets are MVN Partition \(\mathbf{X}=(\mathbf{X}_1',\mathbf{X}_2')'\). Then \(\mathbf{X}_1\sim N_q(\boldsymbol{\mu}_1,\boldsymbol{\Sigma}_{11})\).
Zero covariance \(\Leftrightarrow\) independence (MVN only) \[\boldsymbol{\Sigma}_{12}=\mathbf{0}\;\Longleftrightarrow\;\mathbf{X}_1\perp\!\!\!\perp\mathbf{X}_2\]
§3.4.4 — Conditional distribution
proof — matrix A given
▼
Result \[\mathbf{X}_1|\mathbf{X}_2=\mathbf{x}_2\sim N_q\!\left(\boldsymbol{\mu}_1+\boldsymbol{\Sigma}_{12}\boldsymbol{\Sigma}_{22}^{-1}(\mathbf{x}_2-\boldsymbol{\mu}_2),\;\boldsymbol{\Sigma}_{11}-\boldsymbol{\Sigma}_{12}\boldsymbol{\Sigma}_{22}^{-1}\boldsymbol{\Sigma}_{21}\right)\] Bivariate: \(X_1|X_2\sim N\!\left(\mu_1+\dfrac{\sigma_{12}}{\sigma_{22}}(x_2-\mu_2),\;\sigma_{11}-\dfrac{\sigma_{12}^2}{\sigma_{22}}\right)\)
Proof — Stefan: "I would always tell you: using A defined as such, derive the distribution."
  1. Define \(\mathbf{A}=\begin{pmatrix}\mathbf{I}_q & -\boldsymbol{\Sigma}_{12}\boldsymbol{\Sigma}_{22}^{-1}\\ \mathbf{0} & \mathbf{I}_{p-q}\end{pmatrix}\) (will be given in exam).
  2. Apply Result 2: \(\mathbf{A}(\mathbf{X}-\boldsymbol{\mu})\sim N_p(\mathbf{0},\,\mathbf{A}\boldsymbol{\Sigma}\mathbf{A}')\).
  3. Compute: \(\mathbf{A}\boldsymbol{\Sigma}\mathbf{A}'=\begin{pmatrix}\boldsymbol{\Sigma}_{11}-\boldsymbol{\Sigma}_{12}\boldsymbol{\Sigma}_{22}^{-1}\boldsymbol{\Sigma}_{21}&\mathbf{0}\\\mathbf{0}&\boldsymbol{\Sigma}_{22}\end{pmatrix}\). Off-diagonal \(\mathbf{0}\).
  4. By Property 3: zero cov in MVN \(\Rightarrow\) independence. So \(\mathbf{X}_1-\boldsymbol{\mu}_1-\boldsymbol{\Sigma}_{12}\boldsymbol{\Sigma}_{22}^{-1}(\mathbf{X}_2-\boldsymbol{\mu}_2)\perp\!\!\!\perp(\mathbf{X}_2-\boldsymbol{\mu}_2)\).
  5. Condition on \(\mathbf{X}_2=\mathbf{x}_2\): conditioning value is a constant, preserves normality. \(\square\)
Conditional covariance \(\boldsymbol{\Sigma}_{11.2}=\boldsymbol{\Sigma}_{11}-\boldsymbol{\Sigma}_{12}\boldsymbol{\Sigma}_{22}^{-1}\boldsymbol{\Sigma}_{21}\) does not depend on \(\mathbf{x}_2\).

§5.2 — Hotelling's \(T^2\), everything
NB · yes×6
▼
Definition and distribution \[T^2=n(\bar{\mathbf{X}}-\boldsymbol{\mu}_0)'\mathbf{S}^{-1}(\bar{\mathbf{X}}-\boldsymbol{\mu}_0)\sim\frac{(n-1)p}{n-p}F_{p,\,n-p}\]
Rejection rule Reject \(H_0:\boldsymbol{\mu}=\boldsymbol{\mu}_0\) when \(\dfrac{n-p}{(n-1)p}T^2>F_{p,n-p}(\alpha)\)
Structural form (why \(F\)-distributed) \(T^2 = \underbrace{[\sqrt{n}(\bar{\mathbf{X}}-\boldsymbol{\mu}_0)]'}_{\sim N_p(\mathbf{0},\boldsymbol{\Sigma})}\underbrace{\left[\tfrac{\mathbf{A}}{n-1}\right]^{-1}}_{\text{Wishart}/df}\underbrace{[\sqrt{n}(\bar{\mathbf{X}}-\boldsymbol{\mu}_0)]}_{\sim N_p(\mathbf{0},\boldsymbol{\Sigma})}\) Independence of \(\bar{\mathbf{X}}\) and \(\mathbf{S}\) \(\Rightarrow\) \(F\)-distribution.
Invariance Under \(\mathbf{Y}=\mathbf{C}\mathbf{X}+\mathbf{d}\) (non-singular \(\mathbf{C}\)): \(T^2_Y=T^2_X\). Proof: substitute \(\bar{\mathbf{Y}}=\mathbf{C}\bar{\mathbf{X}}+\mathbf{d}\) and \(\mathbf{S}_Y=\mathbf{C}\mathbf{S}_X\mathbf{C}'\) — the \(\mathbf{C}\)'s cancel.

§5.3.1 — Link \(\Lambda\) to \(T^2\) using Theorem A.4
proof
A.4 given
▼
Proof — Stefan: "Use the matrix B and this relationship"
  1. Construct \(\mathbf{B}=\begin{pmatrix}\mathbf{A}&\sqrt{n}(\bar{\mathbf{x}}-\boldsymbol{\mu}_0)\\\sqrt{n}(\bar{\mathbf{x}}-\boldsymbol{\mu}_0)'&-1\end{pmatrix}\).
  2. Apply A.4 with \(B_{22}=-1\): \[\text{LHS}=|-1|\cdot|\mathbf{A}+n(\bar{\mathbf{x}}-\boldsymbol{\mu}_0)(\bar{\mathbf{x}}-\boldsymbol{\mu}_0)'|\]
  3. Apply A.4 with \(B_{11}=\mathbf{A}\): \[\text{RHS}=|\mathbf{A}|\cdot\left|-1-n(\bar{\mathbf{x}}-\boldsymbol{\mu}_0)'\mathbf{A}^{-1}(\bar{\mathbf{x}}-\boldsymbol{\mu}_0)\right|\]
  4. Note: \(n(\bar{\mathbf{x}}-\boldsymbol{\mu}_0)'\mathbf{A}^{-1}(\bar{\mathbf{x}}-\boldsymbol{\mu}_0)=n(\bar{\mathbf{x}}-\boldsymbol{\mu}_0)'\frac{\mathbf{S}^{-1}}{n-1}(\bar{\mathbf{x}}-\boldsymbol{\mu}_0)=\frac{T^2}{n-1}\).
  5. Equating: \(\dfrac{|\mathbf{A}|}{|\mathbf{A}+n(\bar{\mathbf{x}}-\boldsymbol{\mu}_0)(\bar{\mathbf{x}}-\boldsymbol{\mu}_0)'|}=\left(1+\dfrac{T^2}{n-1}\right)^{-1}\). \(\square\)
\[\therefore\quad\Lambda^{2/n}=\left(1+\frac{T^2}{n-1}\right)^{-1}\]
Two-determinants form — bottom of page 51 — Stefan confirmed this is needed \[T^2=\frac{(n-1)\left|\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^n(\mathbf{x}_j-\boldsymbol{\mu}_0)(\mathbf{x}_j-\boldsymbol{\mu}_0)'\right|}{\left|\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^n(\mathbf{x}_j-\bar{\mathbf{x}})(\mathbf{x}_j-\bar{\mathbf{x}})'\right|}-(n-1)\] Computes \(T^2\) as a ratio of two determinants — no matrix inverse needed.

§5.6 — Bonferroni intervals
▼
For \(p\) means at overall level \(\alpha\): \(\quad\bar{x}_i\pm t_{n-1}\!\left(\dfrac{\alpha}{2p}\right)\!\sqrt{\dfrac{s_{ii}}{n}}\)
Bonferroni inequality: \(\Pr(\text{all }C_i\text{ true})\geq 1-\sum\alpha_i\). Individual \(t\)-intervals fail: \(\Pr(\text{all contain})\leq(1-\alpha)^p\ll 1-\alpha\). \(T^2\) simultaneous intervals are for all \(\mathbf{a}\) simultaneously — overly wide for just \(p\) means.
